# Literature review

Purpose: check which parts of the talk are already established and locate the increment.

## A. Negative interest on money (carry tax, demurrage)
- **Gesell (1916)**, *The Natural Economic Order*: stamp scrip, a weekly stamp worth a fraction of face value.
- **Fisher (1933)**, *Stamp Scrip*; **Keynes (1936)**, *General Theory*, ch. 23, discusses Gesell sympathetically.
- Wörgl (Austria, 1932–33): local stamp-scrip experiment, stopped by the Austrian central bank.
- **Goodfriend (2000)**, "Overcoming the zero bound on interest rate policy", *JMCB* 32(4): carry tax on currency and electronic reserves.
- **Buiter & Panigirtzoglou (2003)**, *Economic Journal* 113: Gesell's solution formalised; negative interest on base money removes the ZLB.

→ Settled idea. The talk cites it; it does not claim it.

## B. Interest-bearing CBDC and the lower bound
- **Bordo & Levin (2017)**, NBER WP 23711: an account-based, interest-bearing CBDC can make negative rates effective. **Closest predecessor.**
- **Agarwal & Kimball (2015; 2019, IMF WP 19/84)**: crawling exchange rate between paper currency and electronic money ("clean approach").
- **Assenmacher & Krogstrup (2018)**, IMF WP 18/191: decoupling cash from electronic money.
- **Assenmacher, Bitter & Ristiniemi (2023)**, ECB WP 2811: CBDC in a New Monetarist–New Keynesian model; remuneration, haircuts, quantity constraints.

→ Settled idea. Increment of the talk: works under the actual euro-area constraints (cash retained, Art. 16(8)) and shows the asymmetry (zero rate costs space; negative rate cannot create it).

## C. Tiered remuneration
- **Bindseil (2020)**, "Tiered CBDC and the financial system", ECB WP 2351.
- **Bindseil & Panetta (2020)**, VoxEU, 5 Oct: CBDC remuneration in a world with low or negative nominal rates; tier 1 never below zero, tier 2 dissuasive.

→ The proposal on slide 15 is a variant of this; the talk adds the cap at −c̄ and the "demurrage, never expiry" implementation rule.

## D. Zero-rate CBDC hardens the ELB
- **ECB (2020)**, *Report on a digital euro*: an unremunerated, elastically supplied CBDC would constrain the lower bound to zero.
- **Pirgmann & Wawrosz (2024)**, "Assessing the effective lower bound in the context of introducing the digital euro", *Economies* 12(6), 143.

→ Recognised. The talk gives a one-line floor formula and links it to the holding limit.

## E. Expiring CBDC
- **Kahn, van Oordt & Zhu (2026)**, "Best before? Expiring central bank digital currency and loss recovery", *JMCB* (BoC SWP 2021-67). Expiry dates on **offline** balances to automate recovery of lost funds; welfare is asymmetric around the optimal expiry. Different purpose from stimulus; no comparison with demurrage.

## F. Expiring vouchers: empirical evidence
- **Xing, Zou, Yin, Wang & Li (2023)**, *AEJ: Macro* 15(4): Shaoxing digital coupons, ~US$3 extra spending per US$1 of subsidy; some purchases moved forward.
- **Ding, Jiang, Msall & Notowidigdo (2024)**, working paper: 3.1–3.3 yuan per yuan; no substitution across categories or short-run intertemporal substitution; minimum-spend thresholds create notches.
- **Liu et al. (2021)**, *Management Science* 67(12): low-budget stimulus via coupons amid COVID-19.
- **Boehm, Fize & Jaravel (2024)**, CEP DP 1998: French RCT; one-month MPC 61% with a 3-week expiry vs 23% without.
- **Hsieh, Shimizutani & Hori (2010)**, *JPubE* 94: Japan 1999 shopping coupons; effect on semi-durables only.
- **Consumption stimulus with digital coupons: heterogeneity and policy design** (*JPubE* 2026; arXiv 2507.01365).

→ Evidence on the "cliff after T" is mixed (Xing et al. vs Ding et al.): the talk does not rely on it.

## G. Disintermediation and runs (context only; companion talk's territory)
- Brunnermeier & Niepelt (2019, *JME*); Fernández-Villaverde et al. (2021, *RED*); Keister & Sanches (2023, *REStud*); Becker (2025, *International Finance*), digital-euro holding limits from microdata.

## Where the increment lies
1. Expiry and demurrage as two schedules of the same holding cost in one model.
2. Expiry front-loads more spending but wastes resources and breaks fungibility; only demurrage is an interest rate.
3. Quantitative mapping: voucher-strength velocity ≈ 160%–1,200% p.a. demurrage vs policy-sized rates.
4. Floor formula and the asymmetry argument → contingent-clause proposal.

None of these is claimed as a major theoretical contribution; the talk is a synthesis plus a small model.
